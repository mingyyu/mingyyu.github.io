Every project on this site is finished. That is the point of a portfolio — you
show the thing that shipped, in the tense that says it shipped. Brothers
Library tracks real loans. Feudal Dominion runs inside a chat window. Both
sentences are true, and both leave out the six weeks in the middle where they
weren't.

That middle part is the interesting part, and it has nowhere to go. So: a blog.

## What I actually want to write about

The parts of building that don't survive into a project description. A schema
I got wrong twice before it made sense. A piece of state I thought lived in
one place and didn't. The bug that took an afternoon and turned out to be a
misunderstanding I'd been carrying for months.

Those are the things I'd have wanted to read a year ago, and they are almost
never what gets written down. What gets written down is the tutorial — the
path taken by someone who already knew the answer. That's useful once and
useless after.

I'd rather write the other thing.

## What I'm working on

Go and Postgres, deliberately and slowly. Not because I need another language,
but because I want to be genuinely good at a small number of things rather
than passable at many, and those two sit close to the work I like: getting the
data model right, and keeping state honest under concurrency.

Past that, I'm pointing myself at AI, and I suspect the unglamorous parts
decide it there too. What the model actually sees. What it keeps between
turns. Whether a person trusts an answer enough to act on it. Those are
retrieval problems and state problems and interface problems wearing a new
hat, and they're the ones I want to build against.

## Ground rules

Short posts. Specific ones. No post that could have been a paragraph, and
nothing written to look busy — I'd rather this go quiet for a month than fill
with restatements of documentation.

If something here is wrong, tell me. The address is
[on the front page](/#contact), and being corrected is cheaper than being
confidently mistaken in public for a year.
